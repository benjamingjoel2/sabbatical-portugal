It is the question we are asked most often: Lisbon or Porto? Both are beautiful, safe, welcoming cities on the Atlantic, and both are excellent places to live and work for a few months. But they feel very different day to day. Here is an honest comparison to help you choose.

## The short version

- **Choose Lisbon** if you want sunshine, a big international community, endless things to do and easy access to beaches.
- **Choose Porto** if you want a slightly calmer, more local city with real character, a serious food scene and lower costs, and you don’t mind a rainier winter.

Now for the detail.

## Feel and pace

**Lisbon** is a capital, and it feels like one. It is spread over steep hills along the wide Tagus estuary, with yellow trams, tiled façades and viewpoints at every turn. There is always something on: concerts, exhibitions, festivals, new restaurants. In June the whole city celebrates its popular saints with grilled sardines and street parties.

**Porto** is smaller, more compact and a little more reserved at first. It is built in granite on the banks of the Douro, with steep streets, church towers and the port lodges across the river in Vila Nova de Gaia. People here are proud of their city and very warm once you are introduced. Life moves at a slightly slower pace.

## Cost of living

Both cities have become more expensive in recent years, but Porto is still noticeably cheaper.

- **Rent:** a furnished one-bedroom in a good central neighborhood is roughly a quarter to a third cheaper in Porto than in Lisbon.
- **Eating out:** a good dinner for two costs a little less in Porto, and a coffee is often a few cents cheaper.
- **Coworking:** monthly memberships are slightly lower in Porto.

For a single person living comfortably, we budget from about €2,600 a month in Lisbon and about €2,200 in Porto, including rent. Your figure will depend on the neighborhood and how you live.

## Weather

This is the biggest practical difference.

- **Lisbon** has one of the sunniest climates of any European capital. Summers are hot and dry, and winters are mild, with rain coming in spells rather than every day.
- **Porto** is a few degrees cooler and much wetter from November to March. Winters can be grey for days at a time, and older homes can feel damp. Summers are warm but rarely hot, thanks to the Atlantic breeze.

If you are planning a winter season and need sunshine to feel good, Lisbon has the edge. If you are coming from late spring to early autumn, Porto is lovely and often more comfortable than Lisbon in the height of summer.

## Neighborhoods to consider

**In Lisbon:**

- **Príncipe Real:** leafy, elegant, close to everything, with gardens and independent shops.
- **Campo de Ourique:** flat, residential and family-friendly, with a great market hall.
- **Alfama:** the oldest quarter, atmospheric and steep, best for people who love character over convenience.
- **Arroios:** diverse, lively and more affordable, with good cafés and transport.

**In Porto:**

- **Bonfim:** relaxed and creative, with new cafés and quiet streets.
- **Cedofeita:** central, design-led and well connected.
- **Foz do Douro:** by the sea, calm and residential, with long seafront walks.
- **Boavista:** modern, practical and convenient for international schools.

## Community and social life

**Lisbon** has the largest remote-work and international community in Portugal. You can find a meetup, language exchange or coworking event almost every night of the week. It is very easy to make friends quickly, though many will be other newcomers.

**Porto** has a smaller but growing community, with a strong local arts and university scene. It is often easier to make Portuguese friends here, and the city feels less shaped by visitors.

## Work and internet

Both cities have excellent internet. Fibre at 500 Mbps or more is standard in most central buildings, and 5G coverage is strong. Both have plenty of coworking spaces and laptop-friendly cafés. Lisbon has more choice, while Porto’s spaces tend to be calmer.

Both are on the same time zone as the UK, which makes working with Europe easy and gives you an afternoon overlap with the US East Coast.

## Getting around

- **Lisbon** has a metro, trams, buses and ferries across the river. The hills are real, so expect to walk up a lot.
- **Porto** has a light metro, buses and a compact centre that is easy on foot, though also hilly.

You do not need a car in either city. Fast trains connect them in about three hours, so you can easily visit one from the other.

## Beaches and weekends

**From Lisbon**, the beaches of Cascais and Estoril are a short train ride away, and the wide surf beaches of Costa da Caparica are across the river. Sintra’s forests and palaces make an easy day trip, and the Alentejo is about an hour away.

**From Porto**, the city beaches of Foz and Matosinhos are a bus ride from the centre. The Douro Valley is under two hours by train, which makes vineyard weekends easy. The green, quiet Minho region and the historic towns of Braga and Guimarães are close too.

## Families

Both cities have international schools with British, American and IB curricula, as well as good Portuguese schools. Lisbon and the Cascais area have more choice. Porto can be calmer for young children, with parks and seaside walks close to many residential areas.

## So which one?

There is no wrong answer. Many of our guests spend one season in each, and the cities complement each other beautifully: Lisbon for its light and energy, Porto for its soul and texture.

If you are still unsure, come for a one-month taster in the city that tempts you most, and spend a weekend in the other. You will know quickly which one feels like home.
