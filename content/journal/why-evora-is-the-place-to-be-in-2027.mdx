Every year, the European Union names a European Capital of Culture, and in 2027 one of them is Évora, a small walled town in the heart of the Alentejo. For a place of around fifty thousand people, that is a big moment. It is also, we think, the perfect excuse to spend a season somewhere most visitors only see on a day trip.

## A town that wears its history lightly

Évora has been lived in for more than two thousand years, and it shows, quietly. There is a Roman temple in the middle of town, a cathedral that looks more like a fortress, a medieval aqueduct with houses built into its arches, and a whole old centre listed by UNESCO.

But Évora is not a museum. It is a university town with students in the cafés, families in the squares and older neighbors chatting on benches in the evening. Life centres on the Praça do Giraldo, the main square, and spreads out through narrow whitewashed lanes trimmed in yellow and blue.

Inside the walls, everything is on foot. You can live here for months without needing a car for daily life.

## What the culture year means

As European Capital of Culture, Évora and the wider Alentejo region will host a full year of events in 2027: exhibitions, concerts, theatre, community projects and festivals, many of them in villages and landscapes around the town, not just in its monuments.

For a long-stay guest, that changes the experience in a few ways:

- **More to do,** especially in the evenings and at weekends, without leaving town.
- **New spaces and projects** opening across the region, many designed to last beyond the year.
- **A lively, international mood,** with artists, visitors and locals mixing more than usual.

The programme is still being announced, so we will share highlights with our guests as they are confirmed.

## Life in the Alentejo

The Alentejo is the largest region in Portugal and the least crowded. It is a landscape of rolling plains, cork oaks, olive groves, vineyards and whitewashed hilltop villages. People here are known for their calm, their humour and their refusal to hurry.

A season here tends to look like this:

- **Mornings** for work, with fast fibre internet in town and a café a few minutes away.
- **Long lunches,** which are taken seriously. Expect bread, olive oil, herbs, slow-cooked pork, and fish stews closer to the coast.
- **Afternoons** for walking among cork oaks, visiting a village or simply reading in a shaded courtyard.
- **Evenings** in the square, at a concert, or around a long table with friends.

## Wine, cork and the land

The Alentejo makes some of Portugal’s best-loved wines, particularly rich, generous reds. Many estates welcome visitors, and some still age wine in large clay amphorae, a tradition that goes back to Roman times.

The region is also the world’s cork heartland. Cork oaks are stripped of their bark by hand roughly every nine years, without harming the tree. Walking through a cork forest, with numbers painted on the trunks to mark the year of the last harvest, is one of the quiet pleasures of living here.

Other things to look out for:

- **Monsaraz,** a tiny hilltop village above the Alqueva lake, with some of the darkest night skies in Europe.
- **Estremoz,** known for its marble and its Saturday market.
- **The Alentejo coast,** about ninety minutes away, with long, empty beaches.

## When to come

Timing matters in the Alentejo.

- **Spring (March to May)** is the loveliest season. The plains turn green and full of wildflowers, days are warm and evenings are cool.
- **Autumn (late September to November)** brings the grape harvest, softer light and pleasant temperatures.
- **Winter (December to February)** is quiet, mild in the day and cold at night. It suits writers and people who want peace.
- **Summer (June to August)** is very hot.

## Honest caveats

We love Évora, but it is not for everyone. A few things to know:

- **The summer heat is serious.** July and August regularly pass 35°C and can go well above 40°C. Homes need air conditioning, and daily life shifts to early mornings and late evenings.
- **It is small.** If you need big-city nightlife or a large international community, Lisbon or Porto will suit you better. Évora is about an hour and a half from Lisbon by train or car, so a city weekend is easy.
- **A car helps** if you live outside the walls or want to explore the region properly.
- **2027 will be busier.** Rents and availability may tighten during the culture year, so book earlier than you normally would.
- **Old houses are cold in winter.** Thick walls keep the heat out in summer but also keep the cold in. Ask about heating.

## Where to stay

- **Inside the walls,** for car-free living, cafés and culture on your doorstep.
- **A countryside monte** among the cork oaks, ten to twenty minutes from town, for space, a pool and silence.
- **Monsaraz or a hilltop village,** for views and stars, if you are happy to drive.

## Why 2027

Évora has always rewarded people who stay a while. In 2027 it adds a year of culture on top of its history, landscape, food and wine, while keeping the slow, generous pace that makes the Alentejo special.

If you have been looking for a reason to try the countryside, this is it. Come in spring, stay through the harvest, and let the Alentejo set the pace.
